# Client-Onboarding AI Prompt Kit

## 15 Copy-and-Paste Prompts for Freelancers

Use these prompts with ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, or any AI assistant. Replace the bracketed text with your own details.

---

## Category 1: Intake & Discovery

### Prompt 1: Welcome Email Generator

**Copy this prompt:**

Write a professional welcome email for a new client named [CLIENT NAME] who hired me for [SERVICE TYPE, e.g., "website copywriting"]. The tone should be warm but professional. Include: a thank-you line, what happens next (3 steps), a request for any additional materials, and my availability for a kickoff call. Keep it under 200 words.

**What you get:** A ready-to-send welcome email that sets expectations and moves the relationship forward.

**Customization tips:**
- Replace [CLIENT NAME] with the actual name
- Replace [SERVICE TYPE] with your specific service
- Adjust the tone by adding "more casual" or "more formal" to the prompt

---

### Prompt 2: Client Questionnaire Builder

**Copy this prompt:**

Create a client intake questionnaire for my [SERVICE TYPE] business. The questionnaire should have 10-15 questions organized into these sections: Project Overview, Goals & Objectives, Target Audience, Brand & Style Preferences, Timeline & Budget, and Additional Notes. Make the questions specific enough to extract useful information but not so long that the client abandons the form. Format as a numbered list with clear, simple language.

**What you get:** A complete intake form you can paste into a Google Form, Typeform, or email.

**Customization tips:**
- Add "for [INDUSTRY]" to make it niche-specific (e.g., "for real estate agents")
- Change the number of questions to match your needs
- Add "Include mostly multiple-choice questions" if you want easier data collection

---

### Prompt 3: Discovery Call Prep Sheet

**Copy this prompt:**

I have a discovery call tomorrow with a potential client who [BRIEF DESCRIPTION OF THEIR SITUATION, e.g., "needs a new brand identity for their startup"]. Create a preparation sheet that includes: 5 smart questions to ask during the call, 3 potential solutions I could propose, red flags to watch for, and a suggested follow-up email template I can send right after the call. Format as a one-page reference document.

**What you get:** A cheat sheet that makes you look prepared and insightful on every call.

**Customization tips:**
- Be specific about the client's situation for better questions
- Add "Include pricing discussion questions" if you want help navigating that conversation
- Add "Make the follow-up email warm and relationship-focused" to adjust tone

## Category 2: Scope & Proposals

### Prompt 4: Project Scope Document

**Copy this prompt:**

Write a project scope document for a [SERVICE TYPE] project for [CLIENT NAME]. The project involves [BRIEF PROJECT DESCRIPTION]. Include these sections: Project Overview, Objectives, Deliverables (list 5-7 specific items), Timeline (phases with estimated durations), Assumptions, Out of Scope Items, and Payment Terms. Keep the language professional but avoid jargon. The client should be able to read this and understand exactly what they are getting.

**What you get:** A professional scope document you can send as a PDF or paste into a proposal.

**Customization tips:**
- Be very specific about deliverables for clarity
- Add "Include a revision policy" to set boundaries
- Add "Add a kill fee clause" for project protection

---

### Prompt 5: Pricing Justification Helper

**Copy this prompt:**

I am proposing a price of [YOUR PRICE] for a [SERVICE TYPE] project. The client may push back on cost. Write a professional response that: acknowledges their budget concern, breaks down the value they will receive (list 4-5 specific outcomes), explains the ROI they can expect, and offers 2-3 options if they need a lower price point (e.g., reduced scope, phased approach). Keep the tone confident but not pushy.

**What you get:** A response that defends your pricing while keeping the door open.

**Customization tips:**
- Add specific ROI numbers if you have them (e.g., "clients typically see a 40% increase in leads")
- Add "Include a payment plan option" for larger projects
- Adjust the number of options based on your flexibility

---

### Prompt 6: Deliverable Breakdown

**Copy this prompt:**

Create a detailed deliverable breakdown for a [SERVICE TYPE] project. For each deliverable, include: the item name, a 2-3 sentence description, what the client needs to provide, the estimated time to complete, and how many revision rounds are included. Format as a table with columns: Deliverable | Description | Client Input | Timeline | Revisions. Include 6-8 deliverables total.

**What you get:** A clear breakdown that prevents scope creep and sets expectations.

**Customization tips:**
- Add "Include a rush delivery surcharge column" if you offer expedited work
- Be honest about timelines to avoid overpromising
- Add "Include a dependencies column" for complex projects

## Category 3: Onboarding Flow

### Prompt 7: Welcome Packet Content

**Copy this prompt:**

Write the content for a client welcome packet for my [SERVICE TYPE] business. The packet should include: a personal welcome message, my process overview (5 steps from kickoff to delivery), what to expect in the first week, communication guidelines (how often we will check in, preferred channels, response times), a FAQ section with 5 common questions, and contact information. Write in a friendly, professional tone that makes the client feel confident they made the right choice.

**What you get:** A complete welcome packet you can design in Canva or send as a PDF.

**Customization tips:**
- Add your actual communication channels (email, Slack, etc.)
- Include your real FAQ questions from past clients
- Add "Include a timeline infographic" if you want visual elements

---

### Prompt 8: Project Timeline Generator

**Copy this prompt:**

Create a visual project timeline for a [SERVICE TYPE] project that will take approximately [TOTAL DURATION, e.g., "6 weeks"]. Break it into phases: Discovery & Planning, [PHASE 2], [PHASE 3], Review & Revisions, and Final Delivery. For each phase, include: the main activities, key milestones, client responsibilities, and the duration. Format as a clear, easy-to-read timeline that a non-technical client can understand.

**What you get:** A timeline you can turn into a visual in Canva or include in your proposal.

**Customization tips:**
- Name the phases to match your actual process
- Add buffer time between phases for revisions
- Add "Include Gantt chart format" for a more visual output

### Prompt 9: FAQ Builder

**Copy this prompt:**

Create a FAQ document for my [SERVICE TYPE] business covering the onboarding process. Include 10 questions and answers organized into these categories: Getting Started (3 questions), Communication & Updates (3 questions), Revisions & Changes (2 questions), and Timeline & Delivery (2 questions). Write the answers in plain language that a non-technical client would understand. Keep each answer under 100 words.

**What you get:** A FAQ you can add to your website, proposals, or welcome packet.

**Customization tips:**
- Use real questions clients have asked you
- Add "Include questions about pricing and payment" if relevant
- Add "Make the tone more casual" for a friendlier feel

## Category 4: Communication

### Prompt 10: Status Update Template

**Copy this prompt:**

Write a weekly status update email for [CLIENT NAME] on the [PROJECT NAME] project. This week we completed: [LIST 2-3 COMPLETED ITEMS]. Next week we will work on: [LIST 2-3 UPCOMING ITEMS]. There are no blockers currently, but I wanted to mention [ANY RELEVANT NOTE OR QUESTION]. The project is on track for the [DATE] deadline. Keep the email under 150 words, professional but friendly, and end with an invitation to reply with any questions.

**What you get:** A status update you can send every week in 2 minutes.

**Customization tips:**
- Keep a running list of completed and upcoming items throughout the week
- Add "Include a percentage complete" for clients who want progress tracking
- Add "Attach a link to the working draft" if you share progress files

---

### Prompt 11: Revision Request Handler

**Copy this prompt:**

A client just sent revision feedback on [DELIVERABLE NAME]. Their feedback includes: [PASTE CLIENT FEEDBACK HERE]. Write a professional response that: acknowledges their feedback positively, confirms which changes you will make, clarifies any ambiguous feedback with 2-3 specific questions, provides a timeline for the revised version, and gently reminds them of the number of revision rounds remaining. Keep the tone collaborative, not defensive.

**What you get:** A response that handles revisions professionally and keeps the project moving.

**Customization tips:**
- Always acknowledge feedback positively, even if it is frustrating
- Add "Include a visual mockup of the proposed changes" if you can
- Add "Remind them of the scope agreement" if feedback is outside scope

---

### Prompt 12: Feedback Summary

**Copy this prompt:**

I received feedback from [CLIENT NAME] on [DELIVERABLE NAME]. Here is their raw feedback: [PASTE FEEDBACK]. Organize this feedback into 3 categories: Must Fix (critical issues), Should Fix (important improvements), and Nice to Have (optional enhancements). For each item, write a one-sentence action plan. Then write a short summary email to send back to the client confirming my understanding of their feedback.

**What you get:** A structured way to process messy client feedback into clear action items.

**Customization tips:**
- Add "Include a priority ranking" for complex feedback
- Add "Flag any feedback that conflicts with the original scope" to protect yourself
- Use this after every round of feedback to stay organized

## Category 5: Wrap-Up

### Prompt 13: Project Recap Email

**Copy this prompt:**

Write a project completion email for [CLIENT NAME] wrapping up the [PROJECT NAME] project. Include: a thank-you for their trust, a recap of what we delivered (list 3-5 key outcomes), any results or metrics if available, instructions for accessing final files, information about ongoing support or maintenance if applicable, and an invitation for future work. Keep the tone warm and celebratory. This email should make them feel good about the investment they made.

**What you get:** A professional wrap-up email that strengthens the relationship for future work.

**Customization tips:**
- Include specific results if you have them (e.g., "The new website has increased conversions by 25%")
- Add "Include a request for a LinkedIn recommendation" to build social proof
- Add "Mention a related service they might need next" for natural upselling

---

### Prompt 14: Testimonial Request

**Copy this prompt:**

Write a testimonial request email for [CLIENT NAME] who I just completed a [SERVICE TYPE] project for. The email should: thank them for the partnership, mention that their feedback would mean a lot to my business, make it easy by providing 3 specific questions they can answer instead of writing from scratch (What was the problem you needed solved? How did my work help? What results have you seen?), and offer to return the favor with a testimonial or referral for their business. Keep it under 150 words.

**What you get:** A testimonial request that gets responses because it makes it easy.

**Customization tips:**
- Send this 3-5 days after project completion when satisfaction is highest
- Add "Offer a $50 referral bonus" to incentivize referrals
- Add "Include a link to my Google Business Profile" for review collection

---

### Prompt 15: Referral Ask

**Copy this prompt:**

Write a referral request email for [CLIENT NAME] who just had a great experience with my [SERVICE TYPE] work. The email should: express gratitude for the project, explain that referrals are how I grow my business, describe the type of client I am looking for (be specific: industry, business size, the problem they need solved), offer something in return (e.g., 10% off their next project, a free consultation), and make it easy by providing a one-sentence description they can forward to potential referrals. Keep it casual and genuine.

**What you get:** A referral request that turns happy clients into your sales team.

**Customization tips:**
- Only send this after a genuinely positive project completion
- Add "Include a case study link they can forward" for credibility
- Add "Create 3 different versions for different referral scenarios"
