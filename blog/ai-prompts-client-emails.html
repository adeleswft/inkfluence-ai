10 AI Prompts That Write
Your Client Emails For You

Stop spending 30 minutes per client email. These copy-paste prompts generate professional, personalized emails in under 60 seconds.

5 min read · August 2026 · By Inkfluence AI

Every freelancer knows the pain: a client sends a vague update, and you spend 20 minutes crafting the "perfect" response. Multiply that by 8 clients, and you've lost 2.5 hours to email alone.

AI changes this completely. With the right prompt, you can generate a professional, on-brand client email in seconds. Here are 10 prompts that actually work, organized by the most common freelance email scenarios.

1. The Welcome Email

Send this immediately after a new client signs up. It sets expectations and makes them feel taken care of.

Copy-Paste Prompt

Write a warm, professional welcome email for a new client who just signed up for [SERVICE TYPE]. Include: (1) a thank-you, (2) what happens next, (3) a link to the intake form, (4) expected timeline, and (5) my contact info. Keep it under 150 words. Tone: friendly but professional.

2. The Project Scope Summary

After a discovery call, send this to confirm everyone is on the same page before work begins.

Copy-Paste Prompt

Based on these discovery call notes: [PASTE NOTES], write a project scope summary email. Include: (1) the client's main goal, (2) deliverables, (3) timeline, (4) number of revision rounds, (5) total investment. Keep it clear and professional. No jargon.

3. The Status Update

Send weekly or bi-weekly to keep clients informed without them having to ask.

Copy-Paste Prompt

Write a brief project status update email. Completed this week: [LIST]. Upcoming next week: [LIST]. Any blockers or questions: [LIST]. Keep it under 100 words. Tone: confident and proactive.

Pro tip: Save these prompts as text shortcuts on your phone. On iOS, go to Settings > General > Keyboard > Text Replacement. On Android, use Gboard's personal dictionary. Now you can trigger any prompt with a 3-character shortcut.

4. The Revision Request

When a client asks for changes, respond with clarity and professionalism.

Copy-Paste Prompt

Write a professional email acknowledging a client's revision request. Their feedback: [PASTE FEEDBACK]. Confirm I understand the changes, provide a timeline for the revision, and ask if there's anything else they'd like adjusted. Tone: helpful, not defensive.

5. The Invoice Reminder

Gentle, professional, and effective. No awkwardness required.

Copy-Paste Prompt

Write a polite invoice reminder email. Invoice number: [NUMBER]. Amount: [AMOUNT]. Due date: [DATE]. Days overdue: [NUMBER]. Keep it friendly and assume they simply forgot. Include a link to pay. Under 80 words.

6. The Project Completion

Celebrate the win and transition into the next phase (testimonial, referral, or upsell).

Copy-Paste Prompt

Write a project completion email for a client. Summarize what we delivered, the results achieved, and thank them for the partnership. Include: (1) a request for a testimonial, (2) a mention of referral benefits, (3) an offer for the next phase of work. Warm and celebratory tone.

7. The Follow-Up After No Response

When a client ghosts you after sending a proposal or deliverable.

Copy-Paste Prompt

Write a gentle follow-up email for a client who hasn't responded to my last message sent [NUMBER] days ago. Don't be pushy. Acknowledge they're busy, re-state the key question or next step, and offer to hop on a quick call. Under 80 words.

8. The Testimonial Request

Most clients are happy to help — they just need a nudge and a template.

Copy-Paste Prompt

Write a testimonial request email for a client. Our project: [DESCRIPTION]. Their result: [RESULT]. Make it easy for them by including: (1) 3 guiding questions they can answer, (2) an option to let me draft it based on a quick call, (3) where the testimonial will be used. Grateful tone.

9. The Scope Creep Boundary

When a client asks for "just one more thing" that's outside the original scope.

Copy-Paste Prompt

Write a professional email addressing scope creep. The client asked for [REQUEST] which is outside our agreed scope of [ORIGINAL SCOPE]. Be warm but clear: (1) acknowledge the request, (2) explain it is beyond the current scope, (3) provide a quote for the addition, (4) offer to proceed if they approve. Not confrontational, helpful.

10. The Re-Engagement

Reach out to past clients who haven't hired you in a while.

Copy-Paste Prompt

Write a re-engagement email to a past client. Our last project together: [DESCRIPTION] completed [TIMEFRAME] ago. Mention a new service or capability I have added since then. Keep it casual, not salesy. Include a genuine compliment about their business. Under 100 words.
